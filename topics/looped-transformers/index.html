Looped transformers, stage by stage

What if a model could think longer by running the same layers again? Inkling 2 Mini stores 24 layers and runs the middle 12 three times, so every token makes 48 layer visits. These three articles take it apart one stage at a time, starting from the plain machinery. Each stage has its story, its intuition and its math with real shapes, and a 3D scene beside the text that shows the same stage at the same moment.

Article C · 24 beats · start here One sentence through a modern transformer Follow “The capital of France is” through a real small model, with recorded numbers at every step: RMSNorm, grouped-query attention, query-key normalisation, rotary positions, the SwiGLU feed-forward network, the 151,936 scores of the head, picking and looking up a token, the KV cache, and one decode step. Then multi-head latent attention, with real numbers from a real MLA layer. Try: open it, press Play with Narrate on, and watch the attention heat map fill in. Article A · 18 beats Why loop a transformer? Depth as a chain, two ways to add it, a short history, a toy model that follows one hop per pass, two ways to feed a pass, the message gate with real numbers, why loops blow up, and what the bill is: storage, compute, bandwidth, cache and waiting. Try: open it, press Play and watch the red cube go round the loop three times. Article B · 15 chapters Inside Inkling 2 Mini Every component from token IDs to logits: attention residuals, a sliding-window and a full-attention layer, the mixture of experts, the loop in full with our recorded gate values, the cache arithmetic, and the drafter. Try: jump to the recorded run and compare the three tables with what Article A predicted.

Three ways to read, one state

Every page has one state, (which beat, how far through it), and three ways to move it. Scroll reads like an article and the scene follows. Step (the buttons or the arrow keys) jumps beat by beat. Play advances at reading pace. Because all three write the same state, text and scene cannot drift. If you scroll by hand while Step or Play is running, control returns to Scroll at the place you scrolled to. Every beat has its own link, so you can send someone straight to one.

Listen instead

Tick 🔊 Narrate under the scene and the page reads each beat aloud with your browser's built-in voices. On a Mac those are the same voices as the system's. Pick a Voice and a Rate next to it. While it speaks, the sentence being read is highlighted, the objects it names light up in the scene, and with Step or Play the scene moves along as the voice goes. Equations are read as words, not symbols. Scrolling, clicking in the text or unticking the box stops it. Voice choice depends on your browser and system, and some browsers need one click on the page before they will speak.

What is measured and what is not

  • The real model. We ran the official checkpoint on an Apple M4 Max CPU, with Transformers dequantising its 4-bit and 8-bit expert weights to BF16. One 43-token prompt, so the recorded gate values, message weights, expert overlap and timings show what the mechanism does there and are not a benchmark.
  • The toy. A tiny looped model, trained offline on a pointer-chasing task and ported to JavaScript, runs live in Article A. It is deliberately simplified. Both of its variants learn the task, so it shows the plumbing and cannot say which design is better.
  • Citations. Every number and quotation in the prose carries a fact ID, and a checker compares it with its source: the model's config, a committed data file, a line of the Transformers source at the pinned commit, or a published page. Where a figure is second-hand or our own arithmetic, the text says so.
